Tutorial round: market making

EMERALDS has a fixed fair value of 10,000, while TOMATOES has to be estimated from the order book. We wrote two market-making versions:

  • Tight quoting. The fair value is the midpoint of the deepest bid and ask visible in the order book. We quote just 2 ticks on either side, with no adjustment for inventory, and use the full position limit on both sides.
  • Second-level fair value with penny quotes. The fair value is the midpoint of the second level of the order book, which is more stable, while we quote one tick better than the best quotes to get filled more often. TOMATOES trading works in three phases. Take: buy or sell against orders at least one tick better than fair value. Clear: once inventory reaches ±60, unload at fair value so we never sit at the 80-unit limit. Make: post quotes one tick inside the best quotes with all the remaining capacity.

The key insight was about who we were trading against. The market maker quoting the second level of the book was never actually traded with, and all fills happened against the market maker at the best level. So we used the second-level midpoint as a stable fair value, while competing with the best-level quotes to get filled.
